# Working notes (not published)

Built 22 Nov 2026 from: Ejak's literature notes (Pietrow 2024 Snippet, Nooteboom 1940, Onvlee 1973), the Gudang's verified Sumba timeline (History Timeline Canvas, R2), and open-access books read online (Hägerdal 2012, Forth 1981, Nooteboom 1940, Swellengrebel 1978, Onvlee 1973, Adams 1970, Büttikofer 1892).

## Leads not used (only from a non-academic site; check before adding)
asiantextilestudies.com (Sumba parts 1–2) gives a detailed chronology citing De Roo van Alderwerelt 1906 (TBG 48), Kapita 1976, Wellem 2004, Roos 1872, ten Kate 1894. Worth checking against those primary texts, then adding:
- 1636: VOC schooner *Maria* wrecked on "Sandelbosch Eyland" (Ross 1906 p. 188).
- 1662 and 1675: Bima claims Sumba "of old"; offers a sandalwood contract if the VOC drops claims (De Roo 1906 pp. 189–191).
- 1708 / 1718 / 1726: Topass offers and interventions; Topass alliance with Umalulu (De Matos 1974; Needham 1987 p. 5; De Roo 1906).
- 1751: Van den Burgh visit to eight domains (Patawang, Melolo, Mangili, Kapunduk, Kambera, Lewa, Wanokaka, Sampareengoe) — Hoskins and Soelarto say c. 1750.
- 9 June 1756: Paravicini oath ceremony in Kupang; only Mangili's head present (Kapita 1976; Fox 2003; Hägerdal 2012 pp. 376–381 — Hägerdal pages not checked: the OAPEN text read online stopped before p. 376).
- Oct 1758 – 25 Jan 1759: Erasmus mission to Umalulu (Leupe 1879).
- 1768–1770: Corporal J. J. van Nijmegen at Mangili, first VOC man stationed on Sumba.
- 1820: ship *Pamanukan* wrecked at Lamboya, crew enslaved (De Roo 1906 p. 240). Relevant to Laboya.
- Apr–May 1845: Resident Sluyter's contracts with nine chiefs (Kambera, Kanatang, Mamboro…).
- 1860 Brocx renews contracts; 1861 Esser recommends a posthouder.
- Aug 1866: Roos installed at Kambaniru by Resident Coorengel; site says Roos left 24 Apr 1873, but Nooteboom (p. 3) dates his term 1866–1869 → conflict, slide follows Nooteboom.
- 1875 controleur moves to Waingapu; 1879 posthouders at Melolo and Mamboro; 1884 civiel gezaghebber De Roo van Alderwerelt.
- 6 June 1891: ten Kate lands at Waingapu on the *Van Goens* (ten Kate 1894 pp. 544, 561). The slide only says 1891 (Büttikofer 1892).
- 1892: Wijngaarden at Melolo (Savunese settlement, school of 40).
- Mar 1906: Lt Rijnders lands at Mamboro; posts at Mamboro and Waikabubak (Needham 1987 p. 28).
- 1912: four onderafdelingen (Melolo, Waingapu, Karuni, Waikabubak) under an Assistant Resident in Waingapu (Kapita 1976b pp. 51–53). Forth p. 12 says nine onderafdelingen later called swapraja — the two are different levels; slide keeps it general.
