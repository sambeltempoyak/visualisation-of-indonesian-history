# Research notes: Sumba story

## Dropped claims and why
- Origin tradition (Malay settlers at Kambaniru/Umalulu, van der Hoop's Neolithic hypothesis): Soelarto only, not peer reviewed.
- Javanese/Majapahit settlement c. 1350 and Negarakertagama canto 14: Soelarto and Pietrow only; Robson 1995 is in the bibliography but I have not seen the text, so no citation.
- Bima's chronicle claims as claim-arrows (Sumba, Savu, Manggarai, Solor, Rote): only Pietrow (a thesis) states them; kept as words in slide 3's More paragraph.
- Portuguese "Tau Marenggi" and English ships "from Mauritius" in the 1510s (Soelarto): the English at Mauritius is anachronistic (Mauritius was not English then); dropped.
- 1660 treaty with Raja Sumbayang (Awa and Suana): not checked in a text I have.
- Beynon "constant warfare" (date unclear in Pietrow), Biditau's exile to Padang, sandalwood sold in Bali at 30 times (Vel p. 117, not found in the text I have), 70 percent adat land claim, 1998 details beyond the cited ones.
- Sumba slaves to Mauritius, Madagascar and South Africa (Hoskins 2002) : not drawn; Bosma p. 113 names Mauritius only for Bali and Nusa Tenggara generally.
- No capital flows for Marosi, Nihiwatu, Djarum or PT MSM: the sources I checked do not name the investors' origin.

## Open questions
- Routes of the 1677 fleets: Pietrow says the Bimanese hugged the coast of Ende and the VOC took another route, but the landing places are unclear; the arrow is schematic.
- Waingapu's founder: Vel says a Pontianak-linked official of the Kupang Resident; Soelarto an Arab trader from Makassar.
- Where did slaves bought by the Negombo (1680) go?
- Exile destination of Wona Kaka's rebels (Hoskins hints at Java).
- Page numbers for Kuipers (p. 31), Pietrow 1677 pages, and Hoskins 1996 spread columns were inferred from OCR; verify.
- Cited but not in my text set: Hoskins 2002, Awa and Suana 2021, Onvlee, Nooteboom, Soeriadiredja, Mross, Forth, Kamuri, Waskitojati, Fowler, McCarthy, Morizcha, Leupe: pages taken from the old story.
